Plain-language rules for every CleanFixHarish episode

Explain the subject so that an attentive eight-year-old can follow it, while adults still feel respected.

- Use short sentences and familiar words.
- Explain one idea at a time.
- Explain every technical or financial word immediately.
- Use a simple house or school example when an idea is abstract.
- Say “the computer system checks who is allowed to see each page” before using the word authorization.
- Say “money left after paying the worker and direct job costs” before using the word contribution.
- Say “a safe saved version we can return to” before using the words recovery point or Git tag.
- Do not list software names without explaining what each one does.
- Repeat the main idea in a new, shorter way after a difficult section.
- Use clear signposts: first, next, if something changes, and finally.
- Keep numbers simple. Explain that percentages are planning examples, not a promise.
- Avoid hype, business jargon, unexplained abbreviations, and long sentences.
- Ask a small hook question every minute to bring attention back.
- Finish with three things the listener should remember.
