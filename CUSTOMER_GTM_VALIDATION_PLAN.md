# CleanFixHarish Customer Research & Local Go-to-Market Validation Plan

**Prepared as:** Customer Research and Local Go-to-Market Validation review  
**Date:** 19 August 2026  
**Geographic focus:** Harish  
**Purpose:** Validate the customer problem, first offers, provider model, trust message, and unit economics before further feature expansion  
**Constraint:** Read-only plan. No code, database, deployment, DNS, or external accounts were changed.

## 1. The validation objective

CleanFixHarish should not try to prove that people generally need cleaners or handymen; that is already a broad category. It must prove a narrower business:

> Harish customers will choose CleanFixHarish to coordinate a clearly scoped home-service job, will pay enough for CleanFixHarish to retain a sustainable margin, and will trust the process enough to return or recommend it.

The first validation cycle ends when CleanFixHarish has either:

- completed 20 real paid jobs with reliable operational and financial records; or
- found clear evidence that the selected offer, segment, message, channel, price, or operating model must change.

The correct outcome is learning, not defending the original idea.

## 2. Known facts, hypotheses, and required Aviel decisions

### Known from the current product

- CleanFixHarish has a public bilingual website and WhatsApp-first contact route.
- The public service set includes handyman, post-renovation cleaning, move-in/move-out cleaning, AC cleaning, and window cleaning.
- The website can collect a basic request with customer identity, contact details, service, area, and description.
- Google accounts support customer and provider/business profiles.
- The owner dashboard can manage leads, jobs, providers, service/pricing content, follow-ups, and read-only viewers.
- The customer request currently lacks photo upload, preferred timing, urgency, detailed property/scope information, and a complete quote/payment/tracking journey.
- The provider experience is an application/status experience; availability, job offers, fulfillment actions, and payout records are described as future tools.
- No verified demand volume, completed-job profitability, public review inventory, or large approved provider network should be assumed.

### Hypotheses to test

- Customers value one accountable local coordinator more than a raw provider directory.
- WhatsApp is the easiest first assisted conversion channel for Harish households.
- Small handyman work is frequent and understandable enough to become the entry offer.
- New-home/move-in setup can bundle several small tasks into a higher-margin visit.
- Post-renovation or move cleaning can produce higher absolute margin but may require more careful scoping and provider quality control.
- Clear inclusion/exclusion rules and an honest price range will increase qualified requests.
- Photographs before quoting will reduce scope mistakes and follow-up time.
- Customers will pay a coordination premium for responsiveness, reliability, and problem ownership.
- Providers will accept well-scoped jobs at rates that still leave a sustainable CleanFixHarish margin.
- Building committees, property managers, new-home owners, and households with time pressure may repeat or refer more often than one-off bargain shoppers.

### Decisions Aviel must make before outreach

1. Which three offers will be tested first.
2. The starting price or quote rule for each offer.
3. The minimum acceptable gross-margin floor per job and as a percentage.
4. What CleanFixHarish promises—and what it does not guarantee.
5. Service area and operating hours during the test.
6. Refund, cancellation, rework, materials, VAT, and invoice handling.
7. Maximum weekly job capacity and who performs each service.
8. Whether Aviel personally coordinates every first-20 job.
9. Which customer information may be shared with an assigned provider.
10. Whether customer/provider photography can be used and what written consent is required.

These are owner decisions; they must not be invented by research participants or by AI.

## 3. Customer segment and Jobs-to-be-Done hypotheses

The test should recruit across several hypotheses without advertising to “everyone.”

### Segment A — New residents and people moving home

**Situation:** Moving into, leaving, or finishing a new apartment.  
**Job to be done:** “Help me get several practical tasks completed quickly so the home becomes usable.”  
**Possible offer:** New Home Setup / Move-in Reset.  
**Risks:** Highly variable scope, price sensitivity after moving costs, jobs span different skills.  
**Evidence to seek:** Number of tasks, urgency, coordination pain, preferred bundle, willingness to pay for one visit.

### Segment B — Busy households with accumulated small repairs

**Situation:** Several small tasks have been delayed because finding and coordinating help is inconvenient.  
**Job to be done:** “Take this list off my mind in one organized visit.”  
**Possible offer:** Home Visit with defined time and scope.  
**Risks:** Travel and setup time destroy margin; customer expects unlimited tasks.  
**Evidence to seek:** Task bundle, urgency, reason for delay, decision criteria, acceptable visit structure.

### Segment C — Households after renovation or construction work

**Situation:** Home looks finished but dust/residue makes it unusable.  
**Job to be done:** “Make the apartment safe and ready to live in without managing a cleaning crew myself.”  
**Possible offer:** Post-Renovation Cleaning after photographs/site assessment.  
**Risks:** Scope estimation, quality disputes, equipment/materials, access, apartment size, provider capacity.  
**Evidence to seek:** How customers currently find cleaners, failure stories, quote inputs, inspection expectations, decision timeline.

### Segment D — Property managers and building committees

**Situation:** Recurring small maintenance and urgent resident issues need coordination.  
**Job to be done:** “Give me one dependable contact and a record of what was requested, completed, and paid.”  
**Possible offer:** Do not sell a subscription yet; test individual managed jobs and the desire for a recurring plan.  
**Risks:** Procurement expectations, payment terms, insurance, service breadth, after-hours demands.  
**Evidence to seek:** Monthly incident types, current process, approval authority, invoice requirements, response expectations.

### Segment E — Older adults, people with limited mobility, or family coordinators

**Situation:** A resident needs practical home help, sometimes arranged by a relative.  
**Job to be done:** “Let me arrange trustworthy help without leaving the resident uncertain or alone.”  
**Possible offer:** Assisted Home Visit with clear arrival identity and family contact.  
**Risks:** High trust and safeguarding requirements; do not target vulnerability aggressively.  
**Evidence to seek:** Booking party vs resident, communication needs, provider identity, safety expectations.

## 4. Ten-customer discovery interview plan

### Recruiting mix

Interview 10 people before treating any message as validated:

- 3 recent/new residents or people who moved in the last 12 months.
- 3 households that purchased handyman/cleaning help recently.
- 2 people who struggled to find a provider or abandoned a task.
- 1 building committee/property-management contact.
- 1 person who coordinates services for a parent/relative or has accessibility needs.

Avoid interviewing only friends who want to be supportive. A friend may participate, but record the relationship and do not treat praise as purchase evidence.

### Interview rules

- 20–30 minutes.
- Ask about the last real event, not theoretical future behavior.
- Do not pitch CleanFixHarish until the discovery questions are complete.
- Do not ask “Would you use this?” Ask what they did, paid, feared, and chose.
- Request permission before taking notes or recording.
- Do not collect unnecessary address, payment, health, or family details.
- Thank the participant without buying a positive answer.

### Script

1. “Tell me about the last time something in your home needed repair, installation, or professional cleaning.”
2. “What triggered you to handle it at that moment rather than later?”
3. “How did you look for help? Please walk me through every step.”
4. “What was hardest or most uncertain?”
5. “Who helped decide which provider to use?”
6. “What alternatives did you consider—including doing nothing or doing it yourself?”
7. “What information did you need before agreeing: price, timing, reviews, identity, guarantee, photographs, something else?”
8. “What did the job cost? What was included or unexpectedly extra?”
9. “What went well, and what would you never want repeated?”
10. “How did payment and follow-up work?”
11. “If the job had gone wrong, who did you believe was responsible?”
12. “Have you used the provider again or recommended them? Why or why not?”
13. “When several small tasks accumulate, do you prefer one longer visit, separate specialists, or handling tasks yourself? Tell me about the last example.”
14. “How comfortable are you sending job photos through a website or WhatsApp? What would make that feel safe?”
15. “What words would you type or ask a neighbor when looking for this help?”

### Concept test after discovery

Show one short neutral description:

> “CleanFixHarish is one local service contact. You describe the job, we clarify the scope and price, coordinate a suitable provider, keep you informed, and remain the contact until the job is closed.”

Then ask:

1. “What do you think this service does?”
2. “What part is valuable, unclear, or unbelievable?”
3. “What would you need to see before trusting it?”
4. “Would you expect to pay the provider, CleanFixHarish, or either? Why?”
5. “For the real job you described, what would make this better or worse than your previous process?”

Do not count stated interest as demand. Count an actual relevant introduction, request, deposit/payment, or scheduled job as stronger evidence.

## 5. Provider interview plan

Interview at least six providers: two handyman/general repair, two cleaning, and two adjacent/local specialists. Include at least two who decline the model or are skeptical.

### Provider script

1. “Tell me about the last five jobs you received. Where did they come from?”
2. “Which jobs are profitable for you, and which look good but waste time?”
3. “What information do you need before accepting a job?”
4. “What causes cancellations, disputes, unpaid time, or rework?”
5. “How do you price travel, the first hour, materials, extra time, and urgent work?”
6. “Which Harish areas and times can you reliably serve?”
7. “How quickly can you realistically accept or decline a job?”
8. “What customer information should you receive before acceptance and after assignment?”
9. “What proof or photographs would you provide at completion?”
10. “How and when do you expect payment? What would make payout trustworthy?”
11. “Would you accept a clearly scoped job with an agreed provider payout from CleanFixHarish? What conditions would make you say no?”
12. “What should happen if the customer changes the scope on site?”
13. “What should happen if the customer reports a quality problem?”
14. “Which checks are reasonable before joining, and which would feel excessive?”
15. “What would make CleanFixHarish more useful than your current lead sources?”

### Provider evidence to record

- Minimum viable payout/job size.
- Travel/setup cost.
- Acceptance and response window.
- Availability/service area.
- Required job inputs.
- Materials responsibility.
- Payment terms.
- Rework rules.
- Job types they reject.
- Willingness demonstrated by accepting a real test job, not only verbal agreement.

## 6. First offers and message tests

Test three offers with stable fulfillment before adding more categories.

### Offer hypothesis 1 — CleanFixHarish Home Visit

**Message A: outcome**  
“Several small home tasks, one organized visit.”

**Message B: responsibility**  
“One local contact from request to completed work.”

**Message C: clarity**  
“Send the task list and photos. Get clear scope and next steps before the visit.”

Test a clearly limited time/task structure. Do not use “unlimited jobs.”

### Offer hypothesis 2 — New Home Setup

**Message A: milestone**  
“Get the new home ready without coordinating every small task separately.”

**Message B: bundle**  
“Shelves, curtain fittings, accessories, assembly, and small adjustments—scoped together.”

Test a package vs custom quote, but keep scope boundaries explicit.

### Offer hypothesis 3 — Post-Renovation Cleaning

**Message A: readiness**  
“From renovation dust to a home that is ready to use.”

**Message B: process**  
“Send apartment details and photos for a properly scoped cleaning quote.”

Do not advertise a fixed price until photos, size, condition, access, and inclusions can be estimated reliably.

### Test method

- Keep service, price, and fulfillment capacity constant while comparing one message element at a time.
- Use unique source/message codes.
- Run until at least 20 qualified exposures per message or two weeks, whichever is later; with small samples, use findings directionally rather than claiming statistical certainty.
- Interview both converters and non-converters.
- Never use fake countdowns, false “limited spots,” invented reviews, or misleading before/after images.

## 7. Ethical local channel plan

Use channels where the offer is relevant and participation is permitted.

### Owned and direct

- Website service pages and WhatsApp.
- Existing personal/professional contacts, with a direct request for an honest introduction rather than a mass-forward chain.
- Follow-up with previous real customers only where communication consent exists.
- Google Business Profile after business details and service process are accurate.

### Community

- Harish neighborhood WhatsApp/Facebook/community groups **only when group rules permit commercial posts**.
- Answer genuine requests helpfully; disclose that Aviel represents CleanFixHarish.
- Do not scrape members, add people to lists, or send unsolicited direct messages.
- Use a useful local checklist—such as preparing photos for a handyman quote—rather than repetitive advertising.

### Partnerships

- Building committees and property managers.
- Renovation contractors, movers, real-estate professionals, and home-organization businesses where the relationship is disclosed and customer choice remains free.
- Local providers who can refer out-of-scope work.

### Paid tests

Do not scale ads until one offer converts organically and is profitable. A small paid search/social test may then validate incremental demand, with a strict spend cap, dedicated landing page, and source tracking.

## 8. Experiment cards for the first 20 paid jobs

### Experiment 1 — Problem and offer comprehension

**Hypothesis:** A Harish customer understands “one local contact from request to completion” without explanation.  
**Method:** Five-second homepage/message test with 10 participants.  
**Measure:** At least 8/10 correctly explain the offer and next action.  
**Decision:** Rewrite positioning if fewer than 8 understand it.

### Experiment 2 — Structured photo-first request

**Hypothesis:** Job photos reduce clarification time and scope error.  
**Method:** For 10 eligible requests, ask for service, task list, photos, area, and time window through the current assisted process.  
**Measure:** Clarification messages, time to quote, provider scope corrections.  
**Go signal:** At least 8/10 requests become quote-ready without a site visit, where the service permits.  
**Stop/adapt:** Photos are insufficient or create privacy friction; adjust prompts or require assessment.

### Experiment 3 — Home Visit scope/price

**Hypothesis:** A defined visit can be sold profitably without “unlimited work” expectations.  
**Method:** Offer to qualified small-task customers with written inclusions/exclusions.  
**Measure:** Quote acceptance, actual duration, provider cost, extras, margin, satisfaction.  
**Go signal:** At least 5 completed jobs, median positive gross margin above Aviel’s floor, and no recurring scope dispute.  
**Stop/adapt:** Two or more similar disputes/reworks or margin below floor.

### Experiment 4 — New Home Setup bundle

**Hypothesis:** Customers prefer a bundled setup visit to separately pricing every task.  
**Method:** Present bundle and custom-scope alternatives to relevant moving/new-home leads.  
**Measure:** Selection, task mix, duration, provider fit, margin.  
**Go signal:** At least 3 completed bundles with repeatable scope and acceptable margin.  
**Stop/adapt:** Every job requires unique specialists/site visits or duration varies beyond a practical range.

### Experiment 5 — Cleaning quote accuracy

**Hypothesis:** A photo/details checklist predicts the effort of selected cleaning jobs.  
**Method:** Estimate hours/cost before five jobs and compare with actuals.  
**Measure:** Estimated vs actual hours, materials, provider payout, issue/rework.  
**Go signal:** Four of five within ±20% of estimated labor/cost.  
**Stop/adapt:** Repeated underestimation; require video/site inspection or narrow eligibility.

### Experiment 6 — Managed-service trust message

**Hypothesis:** Responsibility/process messaging converts better than generic “trusted professionals.”  
**Method:** Alternate honest message variants through tracked local posts/landing links.  
**Measure:** Qualified request rate, not clicks.  
**Decision:** Use the message producing more qualified requests without higher complaint/mismatch rate.

### Experiment 7 — Provider job packet

**Hypothesis:** A structured packet improves provider acceptance and reduces day-of-job surprises.  
**Method:** Send service, scope, photos, zone, time, payout, materials, and customer notes for 10 job offers.  
**Measure:** Response time, acceptance, clarification, scope change.  
**Go signal:** At least 80% of accepted jobs require no material day-of-job scope correction.

### Experiment 8 — Completion and review loop

**Hypothesis:** A personal quality check followed by a neutral review request produces useful feedback and referrals.  
**Method:** Contact every completed customer; resolve issues before asking for a public review.  
**Measure:** Response, private issue reports, review rate, referral introductions.  
**Go signal:** At least 70% quality-check response; review rate is informative but never coerced.

### Experiment 9 — Repeat/rebook prompt

**Hypothesis:** A useful follow-up generates additional work without message fatigue.  
**Method:** Ask satisfied customers once, at a service-appropriate time, whether another task needs attention.  
**Measure:** Repeat request and unsubscribe/negative response.  
**Stop:** Any pattern of discomfort or unclear consent.

### Experiment 10 — Channel economics

**Hypothesis:** At least one ethical local channel can acquire profitable jobs.  
**Method:** Track every lead through completion and actual margin.  
**Measure:** Qualified leads, completed jobs, acquisition cost/time, gross margin.  
**Go signal:** Channel produces at least three completed jobs with positive aggregate contribution after channel cost and Aviel’s coordination-time estimate.

## 9. Source and message tracking

Every inquiry receives these fields, even when entered manually from WhatsApp:

- `lead_source`: direct, organic search, Google Business, WhatsApp group, Facebook group, referral_customer, referral_provider, building_committee, property_manager, contractor, paid_search, paid_social, other.
- `source_detail`: specific group/partner/campaign without storing unnecessary member data.
- `message_variant`: HV_A, HV_B, NHS_A, PRC_A, etc.
- `landing_page` or service offer.
- `first_touch_date`.
- `consent_to_follow_up`.
- `qualified`: yes/no and reason.
- `quote_sent_date`, `quote_value`, `quote_status`.
- `booking_date`, `completion_date`, `cancellation_reason`.
- `amount_collected`, `provider_payout`, `direct_cost`, `refund`, `rework_cost`.
- `review_requested`, `review_received`, `referral_received`.

### Tracking rules

- Use one source of truth in the CRM/dashboard; do not maintain conflicting spreadsheets and WhatsApp-only records.
- Add UTM parameters to website links: `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`.
- Use a short manual source code in WhatsApp templates when links are unavailable.
- Do not use customer phone/email as analytics identifiers.
- Deduplicate by actual inquiry/customer context, not merely clicks.
- Record “unknown” honestly instead of guessing attribution.

## 10. Funnel and operating KPIs

### Demand quality

- Inquiries by segment, offer, source, and message.
- Qualified lead rate.
- Top disqualification reasons.
- Request completeness/photo sufficiency.

### Conversion

- Qualified lead → quote rate.
- Quote → accepted booking rate.
- Booking → completed paid job rate.
- Median time to first human response.
- Median time to quote and booking.

### Fulfillment and trust

- Provider offer response and acceptance rates.
- On-time arrival/completion.
- Scope change, cancellation, issue, and rework rates.
- Quality-check response and satisfaction.
- Genuine review and referral rates.
- Repeat request rate.

### Unit economics

- Actual collected revenue per completed job.
- Provider payout and direct costs.
- Gross margin in shekels and percentage.
- Coordination time per job, initially recorded manually.
- Contribution after channel cost and an agreed value for coordination time.

Do not call quoted revenue, deposits owed, or unpaid invoices “revenue collected.”

## 11. Stop/go rules

### Offer-level go

Continue an offer when, after at least five completed examples:

- customers understand the scope;
- quote acceptance is directionally healthy relative to the other offers;
- actual cost/duration is predictable;
- median gross margin meets Aviel’s approved floor;
- no recurring safety, trust, or quality problem exists; and
- at least one provider can fulfill it reliably.

### Adapt

Narrow scope, change price, change provider, or improve the request checklist when:

- qualification/quote time is high;
- customers repeatedly expect excluded work;
- actual effort differs from estimates by more than 20%;
- margin depends on unplanned extras;
- provider acceptance is low; or
- a message gets clicks but few qualified requests.

### Stop temporarily

Pause an offer/channel immediately when:

- work presents an unaddressed legal, insurance, safeguarding, or safety risk;
- two similar quality failures/reworks occur in the small test;
- the provider cannot meet agreed standards;
- average contribution is negative after direct/channel costs and realistic coordination time;
- marketing requires misleading claims to convert; or
- demand repeatedly falls outside the actual service capability.

### Product-feature go

Build a feature when the manual process has occurred at least five times, its failure/cost is visible, and the proposed feature removes a repeated bottleneck. Do not build because a feature “might be useful.”

## 12. Review and referral collection

### Quality close sequence

1. Confirm the agreed scope was completed.
2. Ask privately: “Is anything not as expected?”
3. Record and resolve genuine issues.
4. Ask satisfied customers for an honest review without dictating wording.
5. Request permission separately for photographs/testimonials.
6. Offer a simple referral link/message without pressuring the customer.

### Neutral review request

> “Thank you for choosing CleanFixHarish. If the service was useful, an honest review would help other Harish residents understand what to expect. Please describe your real experience in your own words. If anything is not right, reply here first so we can review it.”

### Referral request

> “If someone you know in Harish needs similar help, you are welcome to share our request link. There is no obligation, and we will explain the scope and price directly to them.”

Do not offer undisclosed incentives, gate negative reviewers, fabricate reviews, publish private messages without consent, or ask providers/staff to pose as customers.

## 13. Trust evidence rules

### Allowed when true and documented

- Actual local service area.
- Real response/process timestamps described accurately.
- Real completed-work photographs with written consent.
- Verified reviews linked to real completed jobs.
- Provider checks that were actually performed.
- Clear price rules and exclusions.
- Honest issue-resolution process.

### Prohibited until proven

- “Best,” “number one,” “hundreds served,” “five-star,” or specific response guarantees.
- “Verified professionals” without a completed, recorded verification standard.
- Insurance, certification, warranty, or background-check claims without evidence.
- Invented customer names, transactions, provider profiles, savings, reviews, or before/after results.
- Stock/generated photography presented as a real completed CleanFixHarish job.
- Countdown timers, false availability, or false demand notifications.

Generated images may illustrate the service category if clearly used as brand imagery, but they must not be captioned as a real customer or completed job.

## 14. Weekly learning cadence

### Monday — Plan (30 minutes)

- Review current funnel and job capacity.
- Choose one customer hypothesis and one operating hypothesis.
- Set the week’s outreach/interview/job targets.
- Freeze the tested offer/message so results are interpretable.

### Daily — Operating note (10 minutes)

For every inquiry/job, record:

- what the person needed;
- confusion or objection;
- next action;
- time spent;
- unexpected cost/scope;
- evidence supporting or weakening a hypothesis.

### Friday — Evidence review (60 minutes)

1. Count qualified leads, quotes, bookings, completions, issues, and collected revenue.
2. Calculate real provider/direct cost and gross margin.
3. Read customer/provider notes and listen for repeated language.
4. Separate facts from interpretations.
5. Decide: continue, adapt, stop, or run a more precise test.
6. Approve no more than one customer-facing and one operational change for the next week.

### Weekly learning record

| Field | Entry |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis tested | One sentence |
| Evidence collected | Interviews, requests, paid jobs, costs |
| What surprised us | Direct observation/quote |
| Strongest counter-evidence | Evidence that challenges the plan |
| Decision | Continue / adapt / stop |
| Next test | One measurable action |
| Owner | One named person |
| Deadline | Date |

## 15. Suggested four-week validation sequence

### Week 1 — Understand

- Approve decisions and margin floors.
- Complete 10 customer and 6 provider interviews.
- Select first three offers and exact scope language.
- Prepare source/message codes and one CRM record standard.

### Week 2 — Sell manually and carefully

- Publish/share one offer at a time through permitted local channels.
- Manually qualify, request photos, scope, quote, and schedule.
- Aim for the first 3–5 paid completions, not maximum lead volume.

### Week 3 — Fulfillment and economics

- Compare estimate vs actual time/cost.
- Test provider job packet and completion proof.
- Conduct quality close and honest review request.
- Adapt scope/price before increasing demand.

### Week 4 — Repeatability decision

- Reach toward 10 completed jobs with reliable records.
- Identify the strongest segment/offer/channel combination.
- Decide which single repeated bottleneck justifies the next product feature.
- Continue to 20 jobs only with controlled capacity and positive evidence.

## 16. Final validation deliverable after 20 paid jobs

Aviel should receive one plain-language decision report:

- Who bought and what job they were trying to complete.
- Which offer/message/channel converted.
- What customers actually paid.
- Provider/direct costs and gross margin.
- Coordination workload.
- Cancellation, issue, and rework patterns.
- Trust evidence collected legitimately.
- Repeat/referral behavior.
- Offers/channels to continue, change, or stop.
- The next three product features justified by repeated evidence.

Only then should CleanFixHarish expand its feature roadmap or paid acquisition.
